Four ways the game could actually work

These are running, not drawings — watch each one for about fifteen seconds and the loop explains itself. Same twelve regions, same real flags, same gift mapping in all four. What changes is what a gift does.

1 · Wheel of RegionsMY PICK

  1. Gifts grow your slice — send your region's gift and its wedge visibly gets bigger.
  2. The wheel spins every 90 seconds. Bigger slice = better odds, but never guaranteed.
  3. Winner takes a crown. First region to 3 crowns wins the night.
  4. Because a small region can win, nobody ever gives up and gifting never dies.

2 · Survival

  1. All twelve regions start alive.
  2. Every 90 seconds the region in last place is eliminated — gone for good.
  3. The region in danger flashes red with a countdown, so its people gift to save it.
  4. Last region standing wins. Panic is the engine.

3 · King of the Hill

  1. One region wears the crown; a timer counts how long it has held it.
  2. Everyone else gifts to knock it off — the steal bar fills as they attack.
  3. When the bar fills, the crown changes hands and the reign timer resets to zero.
  4. Longest total reign wins. Creates all-night grudge matches between two regions.

4 · Leaderboard + Juice

  1. The board you already have — but every gift now hits.
  2. Screen shake, the bar recoils, sparks fly, the number punches up.
  3. Nothing about the rules changes; only how it feels.
  4. Safest option, but the leader still runs away with it and others give up.

Why I lean to the wheel: the research on live-stream games is consistent — viewers gift to make something happen, randomness keeps everyone playing, and a repeated dramatic moment beats one payoff at the end. The leaderboard fails all three: a gift adds a sliver to a bar, the richest region wins, and it's decided long before the finish. The wheel fixes all three without needing a single new rule for viewers to learn.
